You each came to Critwall for your own reasons, and you each had your own reason for wanting to be further north. What you had in common was that you were all in the same low-ceilinged shipping office on the same grey afternoon, answering the same posted notice: escort work, northbound, good coin, capable hands only.

The man behind the desk was not a man. He was a gnome, barely up to your belt, with ink on three fingers and a smile that arrived a half-second before he decided whether to mean it. Blink Tinderflip. His name was on half the legitimate cargo moving out of Critwall and, you would later suspect, a fair amount of the other kind.

"Two coaches," he said, before any of you had sat down. "North road, to Ringstown. You deliver to a factor there, name of E. Forger. Forger signs, you get the rest of your pay, everybody goes home pleased."

He slid the coin across the desk. It was more than the job seemed to ask for. None of you mentioned this.

"Coach A is a sealed cask. Salvage, under seal, handle it like it's your grandmother. You do not open it, you do not need to open it, and frankly you'll sleep better not having opened it." The smile flickered, just for a moment, then came back. "Coach B is a correspondence case. Papers. Oilskin-wrapped. Boring." A pause. "That one you deliver intact. That one matters more than it looks, and no, I'm not going to tell you why, because I don't ask Forger questions and you'd be wise to take up the habit."

You asked about the road. The smile went out and did not come back so quickly this time.

"That's the part I'm paying for," he said. "The north road's gone quiet. Coaches leaving and not arriving. No bodies, mostly. Just gone." He tapped the desk once. "I've moved cargo through that stretch for thirty years. Something out there is new. I want people on it who can do more than hold reins."

He stood, which did not change his height much.

"Get my cargo to Ringstown. Don't open the cask. Don't lose the papers. Don't be heroes about anything that isn't trying to eat you." The smile came back one last time, and this time you could not tell if he meant it. "And if you reach Ringstown's gate at dusk, don't look at the mountain too long. It looks back."

You took the job.
